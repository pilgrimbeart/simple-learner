# Can more of the same brain learn unfamiliar challenges?

**Draft closing assessment, 2026-11-21: recommend archiving.** The
[project conclusions](README.md#draft-conclusions-for-archiving) state the assessment;
[the final review](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d185-archive-review) records its evidence and limits.

This project investigated whether a small repertoire of local components could form a robot brain that
improves its own organisation during life. Additional capacity should extend what it learns on unfamiliar
problems without a designer supplying a new solution for each one. Evidence, learning time, material,
energy and communication are part of that goal.

The work established bounded capabilities: small circuits learning a food distinction, restricted local
recruitment under an earlier noise model, improved sequence performance with more capacity, substantial
fresh-seed timing gains from rhythm acquisition and tracking, and local bias regulation in an oscillator.
The notebooks preserve each result's model, supplied structure, controls and physical-validation limits.
These capabilities were not demonstrated together as one generally scaling brain.

A recurring lesson is that representation determines which regularities are accessible within a life.
Predictors can share outcome evidence, whereas competing actions need trials or a justified model. Play
can expose useful body information, but predictability and sensory–motor coupling were not sufficient
objectives for every useful adjustment. These lessons sharpen the problem without supplying a general solution.

The central gap remains autonomous selection and economical use of adaptation. The experiments did not
establish a reusable local rule for discovering useful representations, allocating capacity, retaining
skills and converting learned evidence into action across unfamiliar families. Nominal capacity omitted
some material and communication costs; apparent statistical ties often remained inconclusive.

The final action-grid experiment improves collection when the correct food distinction is supplied at
birth. Independent recounting confirms the primary gains, but no setting meets the absolute task,
practical-improvement, physical-eligibility and numerical-convergence requirements. The results implicate
interactions between exploration and sensory control without isolating a dominant cause. This is informed
action diagnosis, not learning or autonomous repair.

The earlier sequence and rhythm results are the strongest reasons to consider further work. They still
leave the discovery, reuse and total-cost requirements unresolved. The local oscillator repair supports a
specific regulation mechanism, not a route already demonstrated towards general adaptation. On this evidence,
no remaining line makes a strong enough case to delay archiving. The project leaves useful constructions,
causal diagnoses and methodological lessons; it neither achieves the original goal nor proves it impossible.

Archiving has not been performed. [PROGRAMME.md](PROGRAMME.md) preserves the historical protocols and
protected-test register; it does not authorise continuation of those queues under the wrap-up request.
