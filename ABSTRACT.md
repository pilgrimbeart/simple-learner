# Can more of the same brain learn unfamiliar challenges?

**Latest result (2026-11-21):** [D184](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d184-action-grid) tests 81 settings of the catalogue
brain's existing knobs. They point to the explorer holding the shared motors when the senses fire (not yet separated from encounter rate): less
frequent exploration, faster motor recovery and a faster rhythm double informed collection on fresh lives, while
sensory gain does not help. The gain misses its practical margin, step convergence and the chip's rails, and no
setting does the job; learning and local autonomous repair remain unproved.

We seek reusable components for a real-time robot brain that recognises an improvable shortfall and adapts
its own organisation during life. Additional brain material should extend what it can learn without a designer
supplying a new solution for each challenge. Local information, limited evidence, learning time, communication
and energy are part of the problem. The [goal and evaluation criteria](README.md#the-goal-intelligence-that-scales)
separate this ambition from the project's exploratory milestones.

The experiments use a simple simulated robot. Hand-designed controllers establish achievable behaviours and
expose missing capabilities; they are evidence for understanding, not the autonomous solution. Small circuits
learn a basic food distinction. Earlier local recruitment repairs a missing connection, under an older noise
model. The earlier growing networks improve with capacity on sequence challenges. Rhythm acquisition and
tracking substantially improve timing performance. These findings support several useful mechanisms, but
combined challenges, noisy changing feedback and reuse of old knowledge remain difficult.

The strongest timing investigation separated representation of cycle position, recovery of trust in predictors,
and period estimation before constructing a literature-informed tracker. It illustrates the programme's intended
method: explain failures causally, compare established mechanisms, and test the resulting explanation.

The present evidence does **not** establish a near-optimal, parsimonious brain that scales generally on unfamiliar
problem families. Some reported ties remain statistically unresolved; informed reference learners supply priors
that the candidate brain may lack; resource and communication accounting is incomplete. These qualifications and
the supporting records are in [the current evidence review](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#review-2026-11-16).

The [noisy-feedback audit](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d73-noisy-evidence) finds useful evidence after matching the
brain's observation restrictions. A stored summary retains a choice signal; a supplied grouping helps its
readout learn. [Learning the grouping](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d74-learned-sharing) under two priors of one
model family established no decision gain, though its interval does not exclude most of the supplied
advantage, and an inappropriate sharing assumption can delay necessary distinctions. That tests two
priors, not whether useful readouts are inherently hard to discover. A separate
[duplication check](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d75-redundant-selection) shows how extra predictors change prior
influence without new information. Privileged reference observations and high-noise tasks with negative
expected meal gains further limit the original control interpretation.

The [resource audit](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d76-resource-floor) shows the present capacity knob does not cap
total material. [Observing the brain](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d77-resource-observation) without changing its
behaviour shows that, on the hardest tested sequence, extra room for the ten colour-event streams
recovers much of the larger setting's gain; the remaining gap is unresolved.
[That comparison](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d78-allocation-rules) confirms allocation as a large lever: a pairwise
contest by mixture weight beats an equal split on fresh lives, though not the original design. It also shows
that a smaller brain has not yet matched the larger design. [Review of the diagnosis](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d79-active-mixture)
finds that family totals hid useful individual predictors and stored weights were mistaken for active
influence. A validated diagnostic on one reused pair finds that removing non-event forecasts jointly
improves current prediction loss; it also exposes more frequent scoring-order errors under the contest.
These results do not establish that the brain needs a new usefulness signal.
[Replaying and correcting selector credit](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d80-scoring-replay) separates a small
direct prediction effect from changes through allocation and later learning. The small closed-loop
check does not demonstrate better performance. [A consistent credit contract](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d82-issued-credit)
now scores issued forecasts and credits their original surviving units. On ten reused development seed pairs,
it does not demonstrate improvement, and the smaller contest brain's performance gap remains. The next
diagnosis [traces actual recycling](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d83-allocation-trace): foreign transfers often
change current forecasts, and some newly constructed units disappear during the same feedback before
issuing any forecast. This separates interrupted use from construction without a prediction opportunity;
neither establishes lifetime memory value. [Frozen-history replay](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d84-future-evidence)
finds later teaching opportunities for many removed units, including most immediately deleted newborns.
[Reconstructed prediction comparisons](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d85-retained-prediction) find mixed
value: only a minority of recurring retained units beat both their surviving stream and a uniform
forecast. But [reducing confidence in the actual historical mixtures](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d86-mixture-confidence)
worsens probability scores in all four checked lives. Weak individual streams do not establish a weak
combined forecast. [Against the same corrected combined forecast](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d87-retained-mixture),
only 336/3653 recurring retained tallies win, and a supplied causal selection prior does not improve
full-sequence prediction. [Asking whether memory serves as parts](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d88-parent-census) for later combinations
finds weakly predicting units build about 6% of new units, and about one in five new units that predict beat the
combined forecast. Deleted units would have offered many partners nothing living duplicates; those
missing combinations [show no clear predictive advantage](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d92-ghost-children),
leaving no demonstrated memory-as-parts explanation of the benefit of extra room.
[Recombining the brain's own forecasts](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d93-room-prediction) finds better sequence
predictions with more room. A hand-informed allocation reaches similar measured performance with fewer final
categorical units on reused development seeds. [Review](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d103-agent-review) confirms
selected numerical results but leaves selection, learning credit and changed observations as competing
explanations. The [latest review](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d120-hj-review) reproduces the completed spare-allowance comparison:
positive but inconclusive efficiency effects in both families. New units often speak, but standalone bit totals
do not bound their contribution to the combined forecast. [Eight passive replays](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d122-contribution-capture) now reproduce the original lives and
measure small, mixed conditional contributions from transfer-associated new speakers on one seed pair.
The result narrows the forecast explanation on those histories without identifying a causal efficiency route.
Matched counts confirm extra global scanning work in the diagnostic adapter. A
[step-back presentation](lab/v/simple_learner_progress_review.pptx) connects these findings to the original goal;
no new controller or broad run is selected.
No retention or growth cure is selected.
The central question remains which useful relations local components can
exploit or discover affordably. Timing generality and retention remain conditional branches in the
[programme](PROGRAMME.md) and [open questions](QUESTIONS.md).

remains a requirement, not a free observer service. [Input reversal](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d140-input-reversal)

**Latest review, 2026-11-20.** [D175](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d175-results-review) reproduces the
shared-reading detector's fresh-draw improvement, while qualifying its hybrid simulation and the robot
coincidence census. Full physical implementation and reuse remain unestablished. The repair stays parked.
The next proposed investigation diagnoses a concrete robot shortfall using existing eligibility traces,
learning writes and motor responses before adding a timing front end. This connects the component work
back to autonomous, economical in-life adaptation; no general scaling claim follows from the bench result.

**Latest result, 2026-11-20.** [D176](discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#d176-robot-shortfall) carries out that diagnosis on the
designed circuit's robot lives. The evidence linking a sensor to a later taste is present and is written the
right way. The learned knob is lost to its own node's noise and its writers' offsets. Even a perfect knob held all
life gains little, because the mismatched excitable neurons relay the eyes to the thrusters unreliably. Curing
the forgetting with existing means lets the brain retain the answer in most lives without eating better. The
shortfall is in action and retention, not in timing or credit assignment, so a calibrated timing front end
would not address it.
