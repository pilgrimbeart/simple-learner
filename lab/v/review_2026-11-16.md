# Research programme review, 2026-11-16

The user approved a documentation rewrite after an interview, web research on LLM research agents, and a
review of this repository. The goal remains an economical, local, reusable brain that improves during life
and benefits from more resources on unfamiliar challenges. The rewrite changes the research discipline and
the strength of some interpretations, not the simulator or the recorded measurements.

## Coverage and limits

The starting repository was clean at commit `9099ade2fdf7a14b3e02630b7b28906b0d937b79`. The review inventoried
46 project Markdown files, 794 logs, 32 text files and two output files, excluding the virtual environment,
Git internals and third-party dependencies. It used a structured corpus scan and detailed reading of the
current programme, summaries, theoretical notes, notebook sections and the raw results behind consequential
claims. The scan of 826 result files excludes `requirements.txt` and the protected outside-set reply.
This was not a line-by-line independent audit of every output value, a rerun of the experiments, or a complete
source-code audit. Missing provenance in an old result remains missing.

The two outside-test Markdown files and `lab/v/outside_set2_reply.log` were deliberately not read. Their
protection is an experimental constraint, retained in the [current exposure register](../../PROGRAMME.md#sealed).
Already opened ladders were interpreted through their recorded results. No new sealed test was opened.

The scientific assessment is recorded once in [D72](../../discrete/NOTEBOOK.md#review-2026-11-16), including
the table of major investigations, numerical examples and qualifications to D35, D54 and D67–D71. Current
summaries link there. Earlier line notebooks retain their dated experiments and gain updated scope statements.
The earlier [2026-11-11 review](review_2026-11-11.md) already identified several related distinctions; this
review does not claim to have discovered them for the first time.

## What the research-agent literature contributes

These sources motivate the workflow; they do not prove that it will produce a breakthrough here.

| primary source | relevant evidence | implication adopted here |
|---|---|---|
| [Si, Yang and Hashimoto (2024), Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109) | Expert ratings favoured novelty of generated ideas, with weaker feasibility and problems in self-evaluation and diversity. These were idea evaluations, not completed discoveries. | Do not let fluent mechanism proposals or the agent's confidence stand in for evidence. Preserve competing explanations. |
| [Jansen et al. (2025), CodeScientist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22708) | Nineteen returned discoveries became six judged at least minimally sound and incrementally novel after paper review, code review and replication attempts. | Review the path from claim to configuration, code and raw result, not just the report. |
| [Schmidgall et al. (2025), Agent Laboratory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04227) | The authors report improved research quality with human feedback in their evaluated workflow. | Reserve human attention for consequential choices; maintain useful conditional work between those choices. This does not justify stopping for every routine step. |
| [Mitchener et al. (2025), Kosmos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02824) | The system uses persistent structured information across literature and analysis cycles, with source-linked reports. | Keep durable hypotheses, evidence and unresolved questions. The report does not isolate memory structure as the cause of better science. |

The earlier web-research pass inspected selected full-text material as well as abstracts. During this rewrite
the primary abstracts above were rechecked; no independent reproduction of these systems was attempted.
The practical choice is a compact investigation record in the existing notebook, not a new agent hierarchy or
a requirement to produce more paperwork. Its effectiveness should be judged by better decisions and clearer
explanations. The operative rules are in [AGENTS](../../AGENTS.md#research-discipline).

## Technical source checks

- [Gneiting and Raftery (2007)](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf):
  primary text on proper scoring, used to separate prediction quality from action value. The application to
  the project's noisy tastes is our conditional reasoning, not an experiment reported by that paper.
- [Lakens (2017)](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/80918653/lakeequi2017.pdf): relevant text on practical
  equivalence and prespecified margins. Used to qualify “tie” and “no harm”; no margins or new pass verdicts
  have been fitted to the old data.
- [Herbster and Warmuth (1998)](https://mwarmuth.bitbucket.io/pubs/J39.pdf): primary text on tracking changing
  experts. Supplies standard terminology and a relevant comparison, not a proof about our growing network.
- [MacKay (2003)](https://www.snn.ru.nl/~bertk/machinelearning/book.pdf): indexed primary-book excerpts for
  chapter 40 were accessible; full-book opening failed in the web tool. The perceptron qualification is narrow;
  this is not a claim to have freshly read or verified the whole book.
- [Adams and MacKay (2007)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742): primary abstract checked. Its assumptions remain
  to be examined before choosing a change-point algorithm for the robot.
- [Net2Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05641), [Neuromorphic Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04441) and
  [Dohare et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7): abstracts or primary-source
  excerpts checked for function-preserving transformations, mixed-feedback design and recycling respectively.
  Their use here does not establish autonomous general growth, free exploration or scalable locality.

The detailed distinctions and conditional calculations belong in [BOUNDS](../../BOUNDS.md), with qualifications
to the mechanism catalogue in [PARTS](../../PARTS.md). The broader background bibliographies are retained as
reading leads with their original verification limits; they have not all been checked in this review.

## Preservation and validation

- The previous [programme](programme_before_review_2026-11-16.md) and
  [question list](questions_before_review_2026-11-16.md) are immutable dated snapshots, including earlier user
  decisions and unresolved suggestions. Paths inside their original bodies are relative to the repository root.
  They are historical records, not alternative current instructions.
- Other replaced summaries and theoretical wording remain in the starting Git commit. Experimental entries
  are preserved with dated interpretation links or addenda. Raw logs, prediction files, generated league tables
  and sealed artifacts are unchanged.
- The numerical scaling milestone was moved from README to GRAMMAR without changing its seven conditions or
  numerical thresholds. It is explicitly distinguished from the ultimate near-optimality goal. Historical
  stage-3 and intervention protocols retain their own definitions.
- Validation checks local document links and section anchors, retained experimental text, unchanged historical
  records and the documentation-only diff. No simulation, physical-validation test or live behaviour demo is
  required for this documentation-only change, and none is claimed as new evidence.

The first validation pass checked 113 local links with no missing files or section anchors. Exact-content
comparisons confirmed both historical snapshots, the four notebooks' retained dated records (excluding the
explicit new interpretation links/addenda), the seven-condition milestone table and the original intervention
record. `git diff --check` passed; tracked changes are Markdown only.

The rewrite deliberately leaves exact near-optimality margins, new equivalence margins and future experimental
budgets unset. They require a concrete claim and protocol; inventing them here would prematurely specify the
research and risk choosing thresholds in response to the results already seen.
