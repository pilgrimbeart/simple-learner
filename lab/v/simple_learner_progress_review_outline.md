## 6. Why we investigated memory allocation

Why we investigated memory allocation
Extra memory is valuable in some streams, but the allocator often sends it elsewhere.
A hand-informed allocation is a useful reference
It recovers much of the larger brain’s sequence performance, but supplies knowledge the brain must discover.
Retention and weighting have not supplied a general cure
Reappearing memories are not automatically useful. Hindsight winners and small replay effects are not causal proofs.
Sources: discrete D76–D78, D93–D108

## 7. The recent test changed one admission rule

The recent test changed one admission rule
Old rule: a donor must delete a unit to transfer allowance—even if it has spare allowance and no unit to delete.
Tested change: allow that spare allowance to move without deletion. Keep the price, donor draw and credit contract fixed.
Both arms: cap 2300 units; sequence and timing challenges; ten opened seed pairs. All-food controls match exactly. This remains a software diagnostic with global scans.
Sources: discrete D109–D118; spare_allowance.py
