## M15: built in transistors, and the chip's supply (2026-10-25 and 26)

- **The open search from HR_31p_pol** (`evolve_30`, 2026-10-24) found nothing surely better: the league stayed. The
  bit's two writes differ (S0's slope 2, S1's 27): drift, not design; a variant with both S1's scored +2.51 against
  +2.28, tied.
- **Built in transistors** (the user's question: are the amplifier symbols honest?): `minimal/spice.py` writes any spec
  as a flat netlist of SKY130 transistors, R, C, diodes and bias sources (Mead's blocks: `chip.py`), trims each element
  against its formula in SPICE as a chip is trimmed, and `minimal/sheets.py` draws it, sheet by sheet, checked against
  the netlist. First in the 5 V devices at the model's +-3 V: a life replayed open loop matched the simulator's spikes
  within about 2%.
- **Closed loop in SPICE** (`minimal/spice_life.py`: ngspice as a library, the body in its own thread, creature.Life
  unchanged), 16 held-out seeds x 2 at +-3 V: behaviour tied with the simulator's (food +0.05 [-0.41, +0.56], gain
  -0.09 [-1.62, +1.39], no life stranded), but **the supply delivered 1.76 uW against the simulator's 11.8 nW, 150
  times as much**: every element is a pair whose tail current flows whatever it decides, with a reference branch and
  mirrored copies, all across the whole supply, where the simulator had counted only the currents into nodes. Thinking
  had cost more than moving (`spice_life_HR_31p_pol_6V.json`).
- **The user's decisions** (2026-10-26): count power as what the supply delivers; set the supply to one SKY130's core
  devices work at, the best for power, speed given up where needed; count parts in transistors, the minimal analogue
  circuit being a baseline for comparing designs at other levels (op-amps, Sepulchre's blocks).
- **The supply, first try: 1.2 V, and a dead end.** HR_31p_pol at birth in SPICE with the low-threshold pMOS
  (pfet_01v8_lvt): 102 nW at 1.8 V, 69 at 1.5, 42 at 1.2, 27 at 1.0 (at 1.0 V the elements missed their formulas: a
  pair's input window narrows with the supply while its steepness is physics'). At 1.2 V the closed loop failed
  (`spice_life_HR_31p_pol_1.2V_lvt_partial.json`, 8 lives), every failing life biased towards S1's colour, a sensor's
  bias and not a colour's. Three causes: the model's rails sat 0.2 V inside what an output reaches; a bug in measuring
  a shifter; and, the one that matters, **the memory leaked**: a low-threshold pMOS passes about 1 pA when off, so the
  two write transistors on each memory node leaked about 2.8 pA each, 0.1 to 0.4 pA net, a drift of about 25 mV/s.
  The bit was gone in seconds. Modelling off-leakage in the simulator was the user's no ("no point in re-implementing
  SPICE"): a memory must be a proper non-volatile element.
- **The supply: 1.8 V, standard pMOS** (the user's decisions): pfet_01v8 barely leaks but needs about 0.9 V across it,
  which leaves a pair's inputs a window of 0.4 V at 1.8 V and none at 1.2 V. `units.SUPPLY` is 1.8 V, a rail half of
  it, a design's typical voltage a third of a rail (0.3 V); capacitance is now the axiom (2.5 pF) and the typical
  current follows (0.3 nA). All 9 elements of HR_31p_pol match their formulas within 0.3%, gates within 8%, and each
  element's supply current the model's within 1%.
- **The memory: a SONOS cell**, SKY130's flash transistor, written through a pulse driver that fires only above a
  threshold (`chip.DEAD`). In SPICE it holds (drift 0.8 mV/s, from the netlist's own leaks); writes are about 15%
  smaller than the simulator's. **A documented anomaly** (the user's decision): writing it needs 10.5 V across the cell,
  from pumps or extra supplies, neither simulated nor in the power, and only roughly in the parts (`AGENTS.md`).
- **The body's pulses match the technology** (the user's decision): a stimulus or a taste is three typical currents of
  the brain's own (0.9 nA on the chip). With the pulses kept absolute while the circuit shrank, biology's noise had
  flattered designs (HR_31p_pol's gain rate 3.26 against 3.02 with everything scaled; 3.00 before): `designs.rescale`
  now scales every node alike, pulses included, so a converted design behaves as before.
- **The simulator follows the build** (`chip.py`): power is the supply's (each element's reference and pair, a gate's
  pair and copy, a sigmoid's second mirror, a tanh's cancelling branches, auxiliary amplifiers, biases from their
  rails, leaks into analogue ground, outputs fading near the rails); parts are counted from the same blocks
  (`minimal/spice.py`'s count, the netlist's own numbers).
- **Closed loop in SPICE at 1.8 V** (`spice_life_HR_31p_pol_1.8V.json`, 16 held-out seeds x 2, about 29 min a life):
  tied with the simulator on every measure, SPICE - simulator: food +0.09 [-0.34, +0.52], gain rate +0.28 [-0.31,
  +1.00], efficiency +0.23 [-0.10, +0.63], no life stranded in either (SPICE gain 3.94, efficiency 2.25, 308 firings
  per 10 s; the simulator 3.66, 2.03, 335). **Power: SPICE's supply 131 nW, the simulator's 130 nW**, within 1%, where

- **The league at 1.8 V**: power 60-176 nW across the designs doing the job, parts 500-570. The simplest doing the job
  is HR_31p (500); the best driver for energy HR_32p_c (555 parts, efficiency 1.67), tied with HR_31p_pol_pruned (507,

  HR_32p_pol. HR_31p_pol: 513 parts, gain rate 2.88, efficiency 1.56, 129 nW.
- **The parts are an upper bound.** HR_31p_pol_pruned's 507: 168 cascode transistors, 80 in preamplifiers, 74 in the
  elements' cores, 47 bias sources, 41 resistors, 30 in gates, 12 bias, 12 shifters, 10 followers, 15 C, 6 leaks, 6
  diodes, 4 memory. That is a literal translation of each formula to 1%, every slope and offset trimmed in; a circuit
  built from what transistors do natively (a pair's tanh at its own slope, a follower-integrator, a synapse transistor)
  might need 30 to 60 transistors. Next (the user's direction): Mead's vocabulary as the simulator's elements, each with

  tunnelling supplies explicit, and a search for the most elegant native circuit: the baseline "if evolution had worked
  on silicon", from which to ask what a circuit must add to know it needs to learn, and to learn.
